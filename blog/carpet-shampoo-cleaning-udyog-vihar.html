What Happens When Shampooing Is Done Properly

  • Pre-vacuum with a beater bar — lifts dry grit that would turn to mud under the shampooer.
  • Stain pre-treatment — coffee and ink get targeted chemistry 10–15 minutes before the main wash.
  • Overlapping extraction passes — each lane twice: inject and agitate, then recover; recovering dirty water is the whole point.
  • pH-neutral rinse — stops the sticky residue that makes carpets re-soil fast, the classic sign of a cheap job.
  • Air-mover finish — cuts the 6–8 hour dry to 4–5 in ventilated floors.

The difference between a shampoo that lasts six months and one that looks tired in six weeks is almost always the rinse and recovery. If a quote sits far below ₹18/sq ft, ask whether a neutralising rinse is included — the sticky-when-dry result is the most common redo request we get.

Reading a Carpet Quote Like a Facilities Buyer

  • ₹18/sq ft with rinse and recovery — the honest number; extraction is only worth it if dirty water leaves the building.
  • ₹8–₹12/sq ft quotes — usually shampoo-and-scrub with no recovery; carpets look better for two weeks, then wick the dirt back up.
  • Minimum billing — most vendors (us included) bill a 300–500 sq ft floor minimum because mobilising machines costs the same at 200 sq ft as at 600.
  • Stain guarantee — ask what happens to the coffee spot that survives pass one; a written 'we return once free' clause is standard from serious vendors.

The cheapest quote usually wins the demo and loses the quarter — wicking brings the stains back within a fortnight, and the floor looks worse than before because the soil pattern now looks deliberate. Budget at the honest rate, section the work if the number is tight, and insist on the rinse.
