Offices Have Different Bathroom Problems From Homes

A home bathroom deals with soap scum and hard water. An office washroom in Udyog Vihar deals with volume: a 100-person floor uses its washrooms 300–400 times a day, so the failure points are urinal scaling, drain odour and high-touch surfaces rather than grout alone. What the ₹800 Per Bath Actually Pays For

  • Chemical dwell, not quick wipes — descaler sits on urinals and tapware for 15–20 minutes before scrubbing; that contact time is what removes urine scale, not extra rubbing.

Odour Control: Why It Starts in the Drain, Not the Air

  • Trap evaporation — weekend-empty offices let P-traps dry out; Monday smells are evaporated sewer gas, not dirty floors — a jug of water down each drain fixes it and is worth telling your housekeeping.
  • Biofilm in the drain line — the pink/black film inside pipes is the chronic odour source; enzyme or quaternary flush treatment breaks it where air fresheners only sit on top of it.
  • Urinal scales — the crystalline build-up inside urinal traps holds odour permanently until descaled; surface disinfection cannot reach it.
  • Deodorisers — used after cleaning to signal clean, never to mask what cleaning missed.

Facility teams burning money on automatic sprays are usually treating symptoms of an uncleaned drain line. A quarterly ₹800/bath service with drain treatment typically lets offices cancel the spray refills entirely — the washroom smells like nothing, which is what clean actually smells like.
