How to Choose: The 5-Question Test

  1. Fixed price in writing before arrival — app screenshot or call confirmation both count; “estimate” does not.
  2. Written exclusions list — utensils, walls, polish, soft-furnishing shampoo: whoever hides these loses.
  3. Verification proof — partner rating screen or police-verification proof, not adjectives.
  4. Damage and re-clean terms — insurance claim path or 24-hour re-clean window, in writing.
  5. Same team or rotating pros — matters for weekly plans; irrelevant for one-time jobs.
  6. A ₹999-cheaper quote that excludes half the scope is not cheaper.

What Happens When the Clean Goes Wrong — the Real Differentiator

  • Platform route — ticket raised, photos uploaded, a different worker assigned for the redo; quality depends on the second draw from the same pool.
  • Specialist route — the same supervisor who ran the job returns, sees his own crew's miss, fixes it, and the checklist note goes into the team's record.
  • Damage claims — platforms route through insurance windows with documentation; a specialist fixes or replaces directly, which is faster for chipped fittings or scratched glass.
  • The pattern to look for — who shows up for the redo, and does anyone remember your home? Continuity is what turns a mistake into a fix instead of a dispute.

Urban Company is genuinely good at the surrounding machinery — bookings, tickets, refund trails. Where it cannot compete is memory: the crew that returns for the redo should be the crew that made the error. That is the specialist's structural advantage, and it is why the pay-after-walkthrough terms work — the walkthrough happens with the person accountable for the list.
