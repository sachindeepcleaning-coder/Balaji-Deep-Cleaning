Phase Three: The Detail Pass That Shows

  • Switch plates and sockets — paint flecks and cement dust sit on every one; wiped and sanitised individually.
  • Window channels — grit in channels scratches glass the first time a window slides; vacuumed then wiped.
  • Wardrobe and loft interiors — construction dust settles inside closed units; wiped before clothes go in.
  • Grout haze — cement film on fresh tiles needs a haze remover, not more scrubbing.
  • Final glass pass — paint specks on glass are scraped with a blade, then the whole pane polished.

Phases one and two make a site look finished; phase three makes it feel finished. It is also the phase fly-by-night crews skip — a flat that gleams but has gritty window channels and dusty lofts was cleaned to two phases. Walk the checklist above at handover before releasing the final payment.

The Three Phases, and Why They Cannot Be One Visit

PhaseWhat HappensWhen
1 — Gross debrisCement bags, paint tins, cardboard, plaster chunks removed; large scrap sweptAs work ends, before fine dust settles in
2 — Fine dustCeilings, walls, grills, AC grilles and every horizontal surface vacuumed with brushes; the phase everyone skipsImmediately after phase 1
3 — DetailPaint specks and cement residue off tiles and glass with the right solvents, machine polish on floors, fixtures detailedLast, once dust is gone

Run in one visit, the phases cancel each other: detailing tiles before the fine-dust vacuum means dust lands on the polished floor, and polishing before paint specks are dissolved means grinding cement into the finish. Each phase needs different crews, chemistry and machinery — which is why a genuine post-construction quote is per sq ft after a site look, not a flat number over the phone.
