Drains, Balconies, and Terraces First

  • Drain covers before decor — leaves and silt cause every “mystery flood”; clear covers, then beautify
  • Flush, don’t just sweep — outlets need water through them, proving flow, not a dry broom pass
  • Parapet and corners — moss starts in still corners; a stiff brush in September prevents October scrubbing marathons
  • Potted-plant trays — saucers breed mosquitoes and stain Kota; raise pots, empty trays weekly
  • One storm test — after the next rain, walk every balcony: dry in an hour is healthy, pooling is a drain job

Damp, Smell, and Mould-Prone Corners

  • Wardrobe insides — air on dry days, wipe barely-damp, dry fully before reloading; camphor masks, airflow cures
  • Mattress edges and piping — humidity settles here first; air, vacuum, extract if musty
  • Grout and silicone lines — darkening grout is soil and can be scrubbed; cracked silicone is a repair, not a cleaning job
  • Curtains and upholstery — damp fabric holds smell; wash what is washable, extract what is not
  • Seepage vs dirt — stains that return after drying, or grow in wet weather, get flagged before scrubbing — cleaning resets dirt, not leaks

What NOT to Do in Monsoon Damp

  • Do not seal a damp room — closed wardrobes and shut bathrooms brew mustiness; airflow first, fragrance last
  • Do not bleach silicone lines — bleach whitens a week and weakens the seal; scrub grout, report cracked silicone
  • Do not mop terrace silt dry — dry sweeping grinds grit into stone; wet-sweep toward the outlet, then flush
  • Do not stack wet laundry indoors — indoor drying loads the air bedrooms breathe; balcony line or dryer with ventilation
  • Do not paint over damp patches — paint traps moisture and peels by winter; dry, flag seepage, then finish
