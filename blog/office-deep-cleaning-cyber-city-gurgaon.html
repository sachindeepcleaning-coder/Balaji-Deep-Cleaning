Cyber City and DLF Phase 2 (Sector 24, NH-48) is Gurgaon's main IT corridor — and its hardest buildings to clean in, not because of dirt but because of process. Gate passes, dock slots, 9 PM windows and 120-seat floors punish vendors who improvise. This is the detailed Cyber City playbook: access first, density math second, scrub third. Compare with the easier-access Sector 44 detailed guide or the corporate hubs overview.

The Access Playbook (Do This a Day Early)

Every failed Cyber City shift fails before 9 PM — at the gate, not on the floor. The sequence that works: share team ID lists 24 hours ahead for gate passes, confirm the loading-dock slot where the tower mandates one, and record alarm and access handover with one facility contact. Equipment rides the service lift after the dock window opens; crews never queue at security improvising paperwork.

Workstation Density Math (Audit Any Quote)

Square feet lie on high-density floors — seats don't. Desk-level detailing (trays, cable-zone vacuum, chair wipe-downs, monitor-arm dust) scales with heads, so crew size must too. Use this table to audit any vendor's Cyber City quote in seconds.

Cable Zones, Pantries & the 9 AM Test

Two zones decide whether a Cyber City clean passes the morning test. Cable zones under desks collect a year of dust and coffee splatter — vacuumed and wiped per bay, never skipped as "IT area". Pantries run first in the shift (degrease, descale, appliance exteriors, bin sanitization) so standup coffee happens on schedule even when floor machines run late. Mid-shift pantry photos on WhatsApp prove it before sunrise.

Glass, Lobbies & Washroom Banks

Tower floors carry more glass per square foot than anywhere in Gurgaon — partitions both sides, frame wipe, sticker-residue removal. Washroom banks (often 4–6 units) get full descaling: urinals, bowls, tiles, mirrors, drains. Lobby touch-points (handles, elevator panels) close the scope. Carpet tiles get vacuum plus spot treatment; full shampoo extraction books as an add-on at ₹15–18 per sq ft.

Night Supervision Protocol

  • Supervisor on call all night — escalation number shared before the shift, not discovered mid-shift.
  • Zone-wise photo check-ins — pantry, washrooms and one workstation bay, timestamped on WhatsApp.
  • Server-room boundary respected — surface dusting only; unplug/move nothing without written IT approval.
  • Morning density walkthrough — facility ticks seats-per-bay, not just rooms, before sign-off releases payment.

Lobbies, Lifts & Common Touch-points

Tower life means shared touch-points: lift panels, lobby handles, turnstile readers and common corridors that no single tenant owns but every employee touches. Our scope closes these gaps explicitly — entrance mats vacuumed, lift panels disinfected, lobby glass polished — and the tick-sheet marks them as a separate zone so they are never assumed into (or out of) the floor scope. Where the tower manages commons centrally, we document the handover line in writing.

Carpet Tiles vs Hard Floors on Dense Floors

Dense Cyber City floors split flooring three ways: carpet tiles under desks, hard aisles, stone lobbies. Carpet tiles get commercial vacuum plus spot treatment every shift — full hot-water extraction books quarterly as an add-on at ₹15–18 per sq ft, because extraction drying needs the floor empty for 6–8 hours. Hard aisles get single-disc machine scrubbing; stone lobbies get pH-neutral mopping. One product across all three is how vendors ruin stone — the tick-sheet forbids it.

Post-Shift Reporting Pack

Facility heads don't walk floors at 6 AM — they read reports. Every Cyber City shift closes with timestamped zone photos (pantry, washrooms, one workstation bay), a ticked 9-area sheet, and an exception log for anything found broken, leaking or beyond scope (a cracked tile, a dead flush valve) with photos. Exceptions route into your facility ticketing instead of becoming next-quarter surprises.
